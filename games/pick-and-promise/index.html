What the game shows

The winner's curse

You compare many candidates on limited data and keep the ones that look best. Part of what made them look best was luck, so the same data now overstates how good they are. In the game the five picked couriers are always a little slower than their tryouts suggested.

Certificates for whole distributions

A single target (“under 15 minutes”) is often not known in advance. The paper certifies the entire distribution of a KPI with a confidence band around its cumulative distribution function. From one band you can read off the guaranteed time at any reliability level, which is what the dial under the screen does.

Reuse, split, or correct

Reusing the data naively breaks more than the allowed one certificate in ten. Splitting the data is valid but throws away samples, so bands get wider and picks get worse. PS-DME reuses everything and pays a correction, computed with e-values, that depends only on how many candidates you looked at and how many you kept.

When is the correction worth it?

The paper gives the exact condition (Lemma 2): PS-DME's band is narrower than splitting whenever the split would reserve less than a threshold share of the data for certification. Picking 5 of 30 is a harsh selection, so the correction is large and the threshold here is about 39%: PS-DME beats a 70/30 split, ties 60/40, and a 50/50 split is a little sharper but has to pick on half the data. Keep more candidates and the correction shrinks.

A. Farzaneh, O. Simeone, “Post-Selection Distributional Model Evaluation,” Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026. arXiv:2603.23055.
